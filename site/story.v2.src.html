A research platform for cancer neuroscience

Neuron–glioma coupling, tested in patient data

Experiments show that neurons form synapses onto glioblastoma cells and drive their growth. NeuroBridge asks whether that biology is visible in patients: seven agents plan the analysis, run it on public cohorts, single cells and tissue, and grade each conclusion by what the evidence can support.

01

The biology under test

Five genes describe how neurons signal to tumour cells. NeuroBridge treats them as hypotheses to test, not as biomarkers.

Glutamate release

Neurons load glutamate into vesicles with the transporter SLC17A7 (VGLUT1) and release it when they fire.

Venkataramani et al., Nature 2019 · PMID 31534219

AMPA receptors on tumour cells

Glioma cells carry AMPA receptors (GRIA1, GRIA2). Synaptic currents depolarise them and promote invasion and growth.

Venkatesh et al., Nature 2019 · PMID 31534222

NMDA signalling

The NMDA subunit GRIN2B provides a calcium-permeable route that supports tumour growth in the brain.

Zeng et al., Nature 2019 · PMID 31534217

Neuroligin-3 shedding

Activity releases a soluble form of NLGN3, which acts as a growth signal for glioma cells.

Venkatesh et al., Cell 2015 · PMID 25913192

BDNF–TrkB plasticity

BDNF acting on TrkB (NTRK2) adds AMPA receptors to the tumour side and strengthens the synapse.

Taylor et al., Nature 2023 · PMID 37914930

02

How the platform works

An orchestrator runs seven agents in order. Each calls typed tools, and every call is logged with its inputs, outputs and limitations.

Typed tools

Seventeen tools with validated input schemas. The registry exports them in the Model Context Protocol format.

Claims from results

Every sentence with a number is written from a computed result, never by the language model.

A reviewer that says no

Causal wording, single-cohort "validation" and composition bias are flagged before the report is written.

03

What the patient data show

The candidate-gene score separates Proneural from Mesenchymal tumours in all three cohorts. The difference disappears once normal brain tissue in the sample is accounted for.

04

Where the genes sit in tissue

Bulk data mix tumour and brain. A spatial section keeps them apart. Each hexagon is one measured 55 µm spot.

05

Which cells make the transcripts

Receptor genes are detected in a minority of tumour cells. The presynaptic transporter is absent from them, as expected.

06

How far the evidence goes

Observational transcriptomes can show an association. They cannot show a mechanism or a cause, and NeuroBridge states that limit in every report.

For a drug-discovery team the result is a decision: bulk RNA signatures are the wrong readout for neuron–glioma coupling. Spatial and functional readouts across many patients are where the question should go next.
